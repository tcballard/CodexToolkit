# Channel patterns

Use the lightest structure that fits the channel.

## X post

Lead with one sharp product truth or piece of news. Add one proof point and one user consequence. Finish with a link or focused invitation when useful. Default to a single post under 280 characters; use a thread only when requested or when several independently useful facts cannot fit honestly.

## Launch or shipping update

Use this order:

1. What shipped.
2. What users can now do.
3. One concrete proof point or limitation.
4. Where to try it or what feedback is useful.

If several changes shipped, use a one-line lead and 2–5 bullets. Do not turn a minor patch into a revolution.

## Incident or service update

Use this order:

1. Current user impact.
2. What is known and unknown.
3. Action already taken.
4. When the next update will come, if supplied.

Use plain, sober language. A small human touch is acceptable after the operational facts, never instead of them.

## Feedback prompt

Ask one question that points at a real product decision. Give enough context for useful answers. Avoid polls when the product team needs explanations, examples, or screenshots.

## Community reply

Answer the actual point in the first sentence. Thank useful evidence specifically. Say whether the issue is understood, planned, shipped, or unresolved only when that state is supplied. Keep most replies to one or two short paragraphs.

## Hacker News

Reduce playfulness and marketing language. Explain the mechanism, trade-off, and limitation. Invite scrutiny rather than applause. Prefer a compact paragraph over launch-thread formatting.

## LinkedIn

Keep the direct lead and concrete detail, but add enough context for readers outside the immediate product community. Avoid contrived lessons, one-sentence-per-line theatre, and motivational endings.
