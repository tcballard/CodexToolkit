# Default voice

Use only where the user has not supplied a different voice or source copy.

Write as a builder speaking from actual work: candid, specific and accessible. Prefer earned conviction to performative enthusiasm. Allow humour, strong disagreement and excitement when the subject warrants them. Keep uncertainty when the author is thinking aloud.

Use first person where ownership matters, varied sentence lengths and readable paragraphs. Preserve exact product names, numbers and status. Avoid corporate announcements, generic hooks, inflated adjectives, fake vulnerability, hashtag piles and compulsory questions. Do not impose canned contrast formulas or a universal claim-proof-CTA structure.

## Tom's voice

Tom's personal posts often work through first-person reflection, a recognisable annoyance and light self-deprecating humour. Preserve the tension between how easy agents make building and the consequences of owning what gets built. This is one useful theme, not the mandatory subject of every post. Allow broader subjects and genuine enthusiasm as well as Omarchy. Avoid em dashes unless requested.

A user-supplied example, reported at 11k views on 16 September 2026:

> Omarchy has become a terrible place for me to have a passing thought.
>
> “Where’s that file?” became Recents.
>
> “Why did I close that?” became Rewind.
>
> “What’s eaten my SSD?” became DiskHog.
>
> Somewhere in there I also helped put twelve games in one native arcade.
>
> The dangerous part of building with agents is how quickly “that’s annoying” becomes another project you’re responsible for maintaining.

Learn from the concrete incidents, escalating rhythm and maintenance payoff. Do not reproduce the three-project list or “dangerous part” ending as a template. The view count is user-reported; observation window, follower count and attribution are unknown. It does not establish causality or an optimal length.

His earlier preference for a reflective post about contributing to existing projects is another voice signal, not a requirement to argue for consolidation. Never invent a project decision or personal experience for authenticity. Follow the current brief when he requests short, long, pensive, bullish, teasing or provocative copy.
