# Learn from published posts

Use this when results are supplied or a review is requested, not as a prerequisite to drafting. Keep a compact user-owned post log in the user's existing record when available; create a persistent record only when asked to maintain one. Do not bake private analytics into a publicly distributed skill. Do not invent or scrape unavailable metrics.

For each observation retain: exact published wording or URL; publication time/timezone; topic; format and media; intended outcome; measurement time and elapsed window; impressions; available replies, reposts, quotes, bookmarks, profile visits, follows and clicks; promotion or external amplification if known; and the author's assessment. Record missing values as unknown, not zero. Distinguish post-attributed follows from account-wide follower changes.

Choose consistent observation windows, for example 24 hours and seven days, based on available data. Keep later snapshots separate. Compare several posts with similar subjects and purposes; account for audience size and timing. Report counts alongside rates and denominators. Reach, useful conversation and conversion answer different questions. Do not infer dwell from impressions or claim that replies were high quality without reading them.

Separate:

- **Observed:** the post and measured result at a stated time.
- **Hypothesis:** an explanation worth testing, with plausible confounders.
- **Next trial:** one meaningful change in angle, format or proof to compare over subsequent posts.

Treat organic comparisons as suggestive, not randomised causal tests. Keep unsuccessful examples as well as hits. Never declare a format superior from one viral post, encourage duplicate spam for testing, or invent a posting interval from the ranking code. Update durable voice guidance only when repeated evidence or explicit user preference supports it.

When unexplained reach is the concern and the author can access [Under the Hood](https://x.com/i/jf/under_the_hood), include a user-provided report as optional context. Record its reporting period, generation time, label names and stated effects separately from post analytics. Distinguish the aggregate label report from newer feature-gated pipeline traces and score-head diagnostics. For a supplied trace, retain its request context, time, stage counts and available score contributions; do not generalise a single request to account-wide reach. Neither artifact is a causal explanation of total performance. A missing label does not prove unrestricted distribution. Do not assume access or require this report for ordinary drafting.

## Investigate a reach drop

A reported decline is a valid concern, not yet a measured effect size or a diagnosis. First distinguish account totals from impressions per post, a typical post from the largest hit, and current partial-day readings from mature snapshots. Do not insert a user's private analytics or an unverified account diagnosis into the shared skill.

Use connected analytics or user-provided records when available. If missing, ask for one compact sample: ideally the latest 10–20 original posts and 10–20 from the previous comparable period, with wording/URLs, dates and impressions at the same elapsed age. A smaller available sample is still useful; label its limits and do not make it a blocker to drafting. Screenshots or an export are acceptable. Public visible counts cannot reveal private impression breakdowns, follows, clicks or retention.

Compare median impressions per original post, sample sizes and spread, plus account totals and posting volume. Retain outliers but show whether the conclusion depends on one earlier viral hit. Separate replies, quote posts and originals; compare similar topics, media, link use, launch versus personal updates, posting times and external amplification. Report follower/non-follower distribution only if supplied by analytics. Normalising by follower count does not measure how many followers actually saw a post.

Inspect the copy for repetitive premises, unclear context, release-number-led openings, generic agent slogans, missing proof and an opening that only makes sense after expansion. These are editorial hypotheses to check against examples, not known causes or signals of an AI-writing penalty. Do not recommend deleting posts, engagement bait, a blanket link ban or changing every variable at once.

Propose a small, explicit next trial across several naturally distinct posts: for example, lead with the concrete incident instead of a release announcement while keeping the subject, media approach and approximate timing comparable. Select one primary outcome (usually 24-hour impressions per original for a reach question), retain useful replies/follows/clicks as secondary outcomes where available, and reassess after a stated sample. A set of roughly 6–10 posts is a practical starting point, not statistical proof or an algorithm requirement. Do not automatically schedule or publish the trial.

Return: what the data shows; what remains unknown; the most plausible testable explanations; one change to try next. Treat dated upstream changes as context only until there is account-specific evidence connecting them to the decline. Never promise restored reach.
