# Learn from published posts

Use this when results are supplied or a review is requested, not as a prerequisite to drafting. Keep a compact user-owned post log in the user's existing record when available; create a persistent record only when asked to maintain one. Do not bake private analytics into a publicly distributed skill. Do not invent or scrape unavailable metrics.

For each observation retain: exact published wording or URL; publication time/timezone; topic; format and media; intended outcome; measurement time and elapsed window; impressions; available replies, reposts, quotes, bookmarks, profile visits, follows and clicks; promotion or external amplification if known; and the author's assessment. Record missing values as unknown, not zero. Distinguish post-attributed follows from account-wide follower changes.

Choose consistent observation windows, for example 24 hours and seven days, based on available data. Keep later snapshots separate. Compare several posts with similar subjects and purposes; account for audience size and timing. Report counts alongside rates and denominators. Reach, useful conversation and conversion answer different questions. Do not infer dwell from impressions or claim that replies were high quality without reading them.

Separate:

- **Observed:** the post and measured result at a stated time.
- **Hypothesis:** an explanation worth testing, with plausible confounders.
- **Next trial:** one meaningful change in angle, format or proof to compare over subsequent posts.

Treat organic comparisons as suggestive, not randomised causal tests. Keep unsuccessful examples as well as hits. Never declare a format superior from one viral post, encourage duplicate spam for testing, or invent a posting interval from the ranking code. Update durable voice guidance only when repeated evidence or explicit user preference supports it.
