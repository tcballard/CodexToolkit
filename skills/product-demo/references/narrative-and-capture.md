## Narrative principle

Build the demo around one visible transformation:

1. Establish the user's situation.
2. Show the trigger.
3. Show the product act.
4. Show the result.
5. Explain why the result is credible or distinctive.
6. End with the product's promise and the next action.

Show one uninterrupted, understandable workflow before decomposing it. Avoid opening with architecture, feature inventories, logos, or lengthy context.

Prefer observed verbs—copy, choose, apply, rerun—over abstract claims such as intelligent, seamless, or revolutionary.

## Timing patterns

For a competition demo under three minutes, start with this budget and adjust to the judging criteria:

| Segment | Time | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Hook | 0:00–0:10 | State the promise and create immediate curiosity |
| Complete workflow | 0:10–0:45 | Show the real product outcome without interruption |
| Guided replay | 0:45–1:15 | Explain the product's role with restrained callouts |
| Differentiation | 1:15–1:50 | Demonstrate important controls, safety, or technical distinction |
| Evidence | 1:50–2:30 | Substantiate architecture, model usage, or measured results |
| Close | 2:30–2:55 | Restate the promise and viewer action |
| Safety margin | 2:55–3:00 | Protect against platform rounding or encoding drift |

For a social cut, retain one outcome and one idea. Do not compress the entire feature inventory into 30 seconds.

## Shot-list contract

Each required shot must specify:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ID | Stable identifier such as `S03` |
| Purpose | What the viewer must understand |
| Starting state | Exact application and data state before recording |
| Action | The user's visible action |
| Ending state | Visible proof that the action completed |
| Capture | Full display, window, detail, terminal, or alternate angle |
| Audio | Live sound, narration, silence, or later sound cue |
| Handles | Stillness required before and after the action |
| Usability gate | Observable conditions that make the take acceptable |
| Claim IDs | Claims this shot substantiates |

Do not list decorative shots without explaining their narrative job.

## Recording checklist

Before recording:

- Set the target display resolution and scaling.
- Disable notifications and hide unrelated menu-bar items.
- Close personal and credential-bearing applications.
- Use non-sensitive fixtures and accounts.
- Confirm the exact product build and configuration.
- Reset the application and fixture to the documented starting state.
- Rehearse the complete workflow once.
- Confirm sufficient disk space and the capture frame rate.
- Decide whether pointer highlighting belongs in capture or post-production.

During recording:

- Hold for one to two seconds before the first action.
- Move the pointer directly and pause over consequential controls.
- Allow asynchronous states to become legible.
- Hold after the verified result.
- Record another take after any notification, hesitation, accidental hover, or unclear state.
- Capture room tone separately when using live narration.

After recording:

- Keep every original take until the final export is accepted.
- Name clips with shot IDs and take numbers.
- Note the strongest take and any continuity issue.
- Inspect the first and last frame of each candidate take.

## Capture quality

Reject or recapture footage when:

- the claimed result is not visible;
- text required for comprehension is unreadable at delivery size;
- the pointer obscures important content;
- private information appears even briefly;
- application state changes between cuts without explanation;
- capture frame rate is visibly unstable;
- an interaction depends on narration to prove that it occurred.
